How to Choose the Best AI Voice Agent for Your Call Center: A Complete Buyer's Guide

By B2B Voice

A contact center voice agent affects callers, data and the people responsible for the queue. Give shortlisted providers the same scenarios and ask each one to produce evidence from the systems your team will use.

Quick Answer

Choose a call center AI voice agent by comparing providers on the same queue, traffic and protected business actions. Verify successful outcomes, staffed handoff and rollback in a buyer-owned pilot. Use a weighted scorecard to compare evidence after setting mandatory gates and defining contract responsibility.

Key takeaways

  • Use weighted evidence ratings plus independent authorization, fallback and rollback gates.
  • Compare operating costs, support and exit terms at the buyer's expected traffic.

What should a call center give every shortlisted provider?

Give every provider the same test brief. Name one queue and a small set of intents, such as checking shipment status, rescheduling within an approved policy and routing complex exceptions. Document the expected business outcome for each. A confirmed reschedule requires the correct new appointment in the source system. A transfer needs a caller connected to the intended staffed destination and an expected context record.

You can evaluate B2B Voice's managed custom implementation using that same brief. Bring the routing map, a set of representative calls, language requirements and the business records your team can verify. A scoped demonstration gives procurement, IT and operations a shared test of the proposed workflow.

Include hours, languages, applicable caller identification and recording rules, incoming traffic and human staffing. Specify how many calls or active legs the center must support during the selected periods. Tell providers who owns the phone route, the application permissions and the fallback queues. Use an anonymized or synthetic scenario set with authorized test records. Give all providers the same opportunity to clarify scope before testing.

Prepare a test packet the buyer can reuse

Reserve a dedicated sandbox with disposable appointments and orders. Include a truth record for each expected lookup or write, a set of different caller phrasings and approved synthetic audio where applicable. Test a happy path and a realistic variation, such as a corrected appointment date late in the call. Give the vendor the business rule and required environment while keeping independent buyer review of the final record. Record which data the staff receiving the handoff can see.

Have procurement hold the acceptance criteria while operations and IT collect observations from their own dashboards. Save the version of the workflow, caller route, language, destination coverage and downstream test conditions. If one provider uses a dedicated line while another uses the actual ACD pilot, record the scope difference and repeat the routing evidence on a comparable path before scoring the page.

Start from the service level the queue needs, then choose what to test. Consider which failures need a staffed handoff and which can safely remain pending with a named owner. Which delivery model fits your existing contact center?

Establish whether the customer experience team can maintain this system and who will respond when it fails. A contact center as a service (CCaaS) platform may already support conversational automation in its routing and reporting. A managed implementation can make sense when the scope needs a delivery owner spanning the phone path, a CRM and a service application. An internal build can make sense when the team can own engineering, on-call response and release management.

Ask for a responsibility map for the carrier, AI application, business-system writes, queue return, incident communication and reversal. A vendor can operate its service while the buyer remains responsible for a failed downstream write. Identify those boundaries in a joint test. For the buyer, a tight integration and visible ownership can be more valuable than a long list of capabilities that apply to a different configuration.

Give the incumbent the same test and service objectives. Procurement should see what each option can complete in the real queue, how it behaves under the same traffic and who is expected to hold the pager. Check whether the vendor is offering a component, a supported end-to-end workflow or a delivery plan whose dependencies the buyer must coordinate.

B2B Voice Call Center Evaluation Scorecard

Use the scorecard to compare eight types of evidence for the same scope. Reviewers should record an evidence location, date, configuration and owner for each rating. Use the same weights under each offer and document any weight change before comparing results. Start with outcomes, handoff and data controls, then include performance, support, costs and the ability to leave.

Evidence ratings use a 0–5 scale

  • 0 means the capability was confirmed absent.
  • 1 means a verbal claim was supplied.
  • 2 means the provider supplied written documentation.
  • 3 means the capability was shown in a controlled demo.
  • 4 means the buyer reproduced it in the pilot.
  • 5 means the buyer reproduced it and reviewed supporting operating evidence.

Leave an unassessed rating blank. Withhold the total until all eight ratings are assessed and the weights sum to 100%. Points = weight in percent × rating ÷ 5. Add the points to get a score out of 100.

What should the procurement pilot prove?

Give each provider the same scenario set and a buyer-owned way to inspect results. Record expected and observed status for each action. Test a correctly authorized change, an ambiguous record that needs a person, a closed destination and a dependency that goes offline in the approved environment. A short pilot can find weaknesses and show whether a route meets the scope. Use the tested sample, conditions and limits when presenting the results to procurement.

Check the weakest signals first. A transfer initiated in software may still end in a closed queue. A response-time average can conceal prolonged stalls at peak demand. Ask for the breakdown by route, tool usage, call type and language. Use identical start and end events. Use a scenario mix that exposes differences the buyer cares about. A combined percentile can mask a slower language or a route with more business-system activity. Record the observed distribution separately for the common cases and the complex turns that make customers wait. Verify the completed business action in each group. If the test has too few samples in a language or peak queue to support a comparison, retain the counts and request a narrower rerun before relying on a service-level commitment for that scope.

Have the authorized security and operations owners review the protected-action test, named receiving fallback and actual routing restore. Define an explicit failure for a write or disclosure without authority, an unreachable safety fallback or inability to restore the route. Withhold production traffic while a mandatory gate is unresolved. Review borderline cases with the receiving team

For a fictional rescheduling pilot, one caller finishes the booking after correcting the date. Review the authorized appointment record, whether there is one updated record and what the caller heard. A second call reaches the escalation route during a shift change. If the requested skill group is unavailable, the case must end in the approved staffed alternate or owned worklist. Listen for an accurate description of the next step and check it against the resulting record.

Repeat with two ordinary arrivals in close succession and an approved unavailable system condition. Record whether the center can see that the appointment is pending, then determine the final state without adding another write. Confirm where the vendor logs its part and where the buyer sees the business disposition. The procurement team can use these observations to score workflow evidence and separately sign the mandatory gates.

Make the decision reproducible. Record the number of cases attempted, the actual number that passed and the configuration under test. Save a transcript or recording only under applicable approval and retention rules, and keep enough non-sensitive evidence for the operations team to repeat the check. A requirement reached in a small synthetic set gives evidence for that test run. Review the pilot separately from claims about overall production performance or an availability guarantee.

How should you compare quotes and operating agreements?

Give each provider the same workload, approved actions, integration list and support window. Include both typical and peak traffic so the quote reflects when callers need service. Have each bidder state its assumptions, the work it includes and the work the buyer retains. GAO guidance offers a method for documenting assumptions and a cost baseline for comparison. GAO cost estimating and assessment guide

Ask the provider to explain how the proposed agreement would classify a short inquiry, a transfer with two active call legs and a long tool lookup. Estimate the scope of each offer using your own traffic and staffing records. If the offers cover different responsibilities, list the work each one leaves with the buyer before comparing the signed terms.

How do you turn the evidence into a buying decision?

  1. Agree on the pilotUse the same variants, fallback and business records with each provider.
  2. Review the gatesGet the responsible owners to verify consent, staffed access and restoration.
  3. Compare complete evidenceConsider the score, costs and delivery ownership under the tested scope.

Consider two eligible bids with similar evidence ratings. Ask which center team owns the test number, the receiving queue, the business-system permissions and the procedure for revoking access. A procurement decision can favor the offer with a supported export and clearly scoped responsibility when the weighted differences are small. Record the cost and workflow assumptions that could change the decision at renewal. Set a follow-up exercise to verify routing reversal and data export on the intended contract scope before migration starts.

Hold the decision if a gate is still unknown or a vendor is asking you to extrapolate from different traffic. Procurement can request a narrower pilot when the gap is material. Choose providers on evidence you can repeat, the terms you can maintain and what matters to your callers. Document responsibilities and the reasons for the selection before expanding access or moving queues.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI voice agent for a call center?

The best fit is the provider that can complete your approved tasks under your actual call mix, routing and policies. Compare candidates on the same business records, transfers, failure cases and operating terms.

Should a call center use its existing CCaaS provider or a managed implementation?

Test the same queue and integrations under both delivery models. Compare the licensed capabilities, integration responsibilities and the people available to support and recover the route.

How should a buyer score unproven features?

Leave the rating unassessed until there is evidence. A zero is for a confirmed absence. Withhold the total until all eight ratings are assessed and the weights sum to 100%.

Can a high score override a failed pilot gate?

Hold the decision if a protected-action, human-fallback or rollback gate fails or remains unassessed. Use the score to compare evidence only within the approved decision process.

What should a call center verify under peak load?

Test the actual busy-hour call pattern, concurrent calls and legs, tail delay, integrations, transfer completion and staffed fallback. Check how each dependency and the receiving queue behaves at the agreed limit.

What should I ask for before signing a voice AI contract?

Request tested scope, data handling and incident terms, capacity commitments, restoration procedures, a realistic cost comparison and usable export and number rights.
