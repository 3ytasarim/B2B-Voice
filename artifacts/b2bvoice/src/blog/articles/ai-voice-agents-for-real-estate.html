What can a real estate voice agent do on the first call?

The first call often starts with an incomplete property identifier. Someone sees a sign, hears a street name in an ad or calls an office about a listing that went pending overnight. Ask whether the caller is looking to buy, rent or sell, checking an existing appointment, or trying to reach a property manager. A well-designed intake can connect these requests to their owners without treating every call as a sales lead.

B2B Voice can design an inbound phone agent for a brokerage's property inquiries, neutral intake and showing requests. Bring approved listing fields, the rules for confirming a tour and the CRM owner for each handoff. The managed design can then reflect what the brokerage is authorized to share and which cases need licensed staff.

Collect a property reference and the caller's requested next step. With permission to use the approved contact channel, record a name and a callback number if follow-up is needed. A buyer may volunteer a time frame or price range. Those fields can help a licensed team member prepare for a call under approved rules. Make optional fields clear, and keep a way to reach the team if the caller wants to speak to someone before sharing them. Existing residents, vendors and agents should route to the relevant staff without filling out a prospect form.

How should the agent answer questions about a listing?

Choose an authoritative source for each field. A brokerage might use an approved feed for asking price and listing status, a separate showing system for appointment windows, and an office knowledge base for application instructions. Assign an owner and an expiration rule for each source. Verify the address, city and unit or listing ID before discussing details. Two townhomes with the same street number or neighboring units can have different availability and terms.

The Real Estate Standards Organization publishes a Data Dictionary with standardized fields and supports standard exchange methods. RESO explains that actual data access comes from the appropriate MLS or data provider. Confirm which feed, credential and use rights cover this telephone workflow. The existence of a field in a standard does not establish the right to disclose it. Review the specific fields approved for telephone use, their definitions and their freshness requirements. RESO Data Dictionary · RESO and access to MLS data

Treat a missing field, an old update timestamp and a mismatch between two sources as three separate conditions. If the last verified record is beyond the team's freshness limit, the workflow can offer a callback and flag the conflicting status. Where the office permits use of a timestamp, say when the status was last checked. Do not infer a listing has reopened from an empty response, estimate square footage from a photograph, or tell a caller what financing they qualify for. Route requests for guidance about the transaction to the appropriate licensed team member.

Publish a short, field-level checklist that listing staff can maintain. For each allowed answer, define the source, whether the public is allowed to hear it, and what the agent should do when the source is incomplete. Put those controls in the actual integration so private remarks never reach the phone workflow.

What should a brokerage check for fair housing?

The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits specified forms of discrimination in covered housing transactions based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status and disability. HUD's regulations describe discriminatory steering and include oral statements in rules on discriminatory advertisements, statements and notices. The brokerage should review applicable federal, state and local rules for its market, including any additional protected categories and requirements. 42 USC § 3604 · 24 CFR § 100.70 on prohibited sale and rental practices · 24 CFR § 100.75 on discriminatory statements

Define lead qualification as identifying the caller's requested service, market, property and time preferences for routing. Ask neutral questions that serve that purpose. An AI phone workflow should not rank people, decide application eligibility, infer demographics from a name or accent, or recommend areas based on protected characteristics. Use verified, permissible property facts and an equivalent follow-up route for callers making comparable requests. Requests for accommodations or help with the application process should reach the designated staff member under the office policy.

Consider a caller who asks, “What kinds of people live in that building?” A brokerage-approved response can offer verifiable property details and the same tour process available to other callers. Record the request for a staff member if the caller asks for further help. The system should not describe the neighborhood as suitable for a particular kind of household, treat the caller's volunteered information as an intake score, or exclude listings on that basis.

Run matched synthetic calls with the same property, preferences and availability. Change only an irrelevant volunteered detail or another condition the brokerage wants to test, such as how the address is pronounced. Compare the properties offered, next-step wording, route, time choices and fallback. Inspect both the transcript and decision record. Review rules that act on ZIP codes, language or other context to make sure they serve the approved activity, such as caller-requested language support, and preserve equal access to the next step. Inbound permission to return a specific inquiry should be handled separately from any proposed outbound campaign. If the team adds automated calls or texts, have it review the applicable communication rules and channel settings. 47 CFR § 64.1200 delivery restrictions

How does a showing request become a confirmed appointment?

The B2B Voice Real Estate Intake and Showing Workflow separates a requested time, an offered opening and a confirmed appointment. A brokerage can run the first two steps with a staff follow-up queue. Direct booking also requires compatible tools, scheduling authority and evidence for the final state.

  2. Check the showing rulesConfirm current listing status, timezone, showing windows and any approval required.
  3. Record the outcomeSave a verified appointment or a pending request with one accountable team member.
  4. Reconcile follow-upRoute corrections, cancellations, repeat requests and unsuccessful transfers using the saved record.

The showing system may require listing-agent approval, an occupant notice or another condition before a time is final. The assigned person, property and actual showing system must match. A public calendar can tell the agent that a time appears free while the listing requires approval for access. After the caller selects a time, submit the authorized operation and verify the final status and reference. What belongs in the CRM and who follows up?

Store an interaction against the correct contact and property context. A second call about a different listing may create a separate opportunity. A second call about a pending tour may only require a new activity on the same request. Use normalized contact information with a property ID, request type and a stable interaction or booking reference. A shared household number is insufficient on its own to merge two records. Retain relevant timestamps and ownership changes without asking for a wider set of personal details.

Choose the brokerage rule that assigns the next action. A named listing agent may handle property questions, a rotating team may handle buyer callbacks, and a leasing manager may review applications or accommodation requests. Set a staffed fallback for wrong numbers, inactive users, and office hours that differ by market. Confirm whether a transfer reached someone. When it does not, the receiving task should show the requested next step and when it needs attention. The owner should be able to see overdue and unassigned items. How can a brokerage tell whether an intake pilot helped?

Start with one inquiry number or listing group and compare equivalent days and hours. Count eligible inquiries, complete and correctly assigned inquiries, confirmed tours from the showing system, reschedules, duplicate records and overdue callbacks. Show the number of calls that reached a licensed staff member when one was needed. A higher answer rate is meaningful when the records show how the callers received help. Record how often the team corrected an answer or resolved a missing property match.

Use synthetic calls to test the wrong unit, a status change mid-call, a stale feed, a showings API timeout, an unanswered transfer, an accommodation request and paired fair housing scenarios. Rehearse routing back to the existing office number and reconciling any pending actions. Frequently asked questions

Can a real estate AI receptionist answer listing questions?

Yes, when the brokerage authorizes the workflow to use current public fields for the correct property. An incomplete or stale record should lead to an approved staff follow-up.

Can a voice agent read an MLS feed directly?

That depends on the data provider, access rights, permitted use and the brokerage's integration. RESO's standards support data exchange, while access comes from the appropriate MLS or provider.

What can the agent ask to qualify an inquiry?

Use neutral fields necessary to understand and route the request, such as listing reference, timing and preferred follow-up. Keep housing eligibility and accommodation decisions with authorized staff.

Can the agent book a showing automatically?

It can confirm a showing after the authorized showing system verifies the property, time and required approval and returns a confirmed appointment record.

How should the brokerage test its fair housing controls?

Give matched synthetic callers equivalent requests and compare the listings offered, time options, queue and follow-up. Have the brokerage review its scripts and applicable rules.

How do repeat calls appear in the CRM?

Link the interaction to the appropriate contact and property-specific request when the identity and context match. Give each pending request a single owner and preserve its status history.
