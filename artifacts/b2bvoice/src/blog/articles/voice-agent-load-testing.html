Voice Agent Load Testing: Concurrency, Scalability and Production Performance

By B2B Voice

Voice load testing shows what happens when a controlled stream of calls reaches a voice agent, its phone route, and the systems it depends on. Use it to establish the workload a specific configuration can handle while preserving intelligible audio, timely replies, correct actions, and a usable fallback. A call that connects and never reaches the calendar or a staffed queue is part of the result.

B2B Voice scopes custom voice agent implementations around the customer’s busy periods and business actions. A contact center answering simple routing requests and a service desk checking a live calendar can encounter different limits on the same phone path. Define which calls must complete and what the system should do when a dependency slows down before beginning a test.

Key takeaways

  • Measure offered traffic, admitted calls, concurrent sessions, and verified outcomes as distinct parts of the run.
  • Use steady, spike, stress, and soak profiles to expose different failure modes.
  • Observe the carrier, streaming audio, model, application, and back-end dependencies together.
  • Test a controlled return to normal service and account for queued work and uncertain transactions.

Name the boundary before reporting capacity

Call and capacity terms
Term What it describes Why it matters during a run
Offered arrival rate New call attempts presented over an interval. Calls per second (CPS) expresses the rate of call attempts or starts at a specified boundary. A generator can offer traffic the route never admits. Identify which side of admission the counter measures.
Average concurrency Typical active occupancy over a defined observation window. A quiet portion of the run can lower the average even when a brief surge exhausted capacity.
Peak concurrency The greatest observed simultaneous occupancy in the defined window and boundary. Coarse sampling may miss brief peaks. Use start and end events where available.
Provisioned capacity The resources or sessions configured to be available at a particular layer. A provisioned worker pool does not establish how many callers complete their tasks within the agreed experience.
Provider-imposed limit A quota a provider applies to a route, account, direction, session, or request type. Carrier initiation limits and simultaneous session limits can bind at different times. Validate the values that apply to the specific setup.

Some phone platforms use CPS for a provider-specific outbound call initiation policy. Twilio’s glossary describes its queuing behavior for API-initiated outbound calls. Confirm the relevant ingress or egress behavior on the carrier and product under test. A longer average call can raise occupancy when arrivals remain steady. An unchanged occupancy figure can still hide people waiting outside the boundary you chose to measure. Twilio CPS glossary

Before voice agent load testing, define when a session starts and ends. You may count active media sessions from stream establishment through teardown and separately count calls waiting for admission. Decide whether bridged call legs consume distinct provider sessions. Preserve the definition with the result so a phone engineer and an application owner can compare the same evidence.

Choose a workload the team can safely reproduce

Run network traffic only in authorized test environments with approved endpoints, accounts, and workload ceilings. Begin with isolated components or an internal media loop where that answers the question. Use a controlled telephone path for evidence about the carrier and receiving device. Use synthetic identities and data, and route all external effects to approved sandboxes. Stop the run when a protection threshold or business integrity gate fails.

Give the traffic a realistic mix

Use a busy period the business recognizes. A short directions call occupies the system differently from a lengthy appointment search. Include calls that interrupt the agent, wait for a tool, or request a transfer. Keep the mix stable when comparing versions. Preserve variability intentionally when the question concerns different call lengths or service types.

Choose how the generator presents new attempts. A generator that waits for previous calls to finish is convenient for exercising a fixed pool. It reduces the arrival pressure when the system slows. A generator that follows an independently controlled arrival schedule can reveal accumulating queues and declined attempts. Verify that the generator, network path, and recording pipeline can sustain the offered profile. A test reporter should be able to distinguish an unavailable generator session from an admission failure.

Pick the test profile that matches the risk

Workload profiles and their evidence
Profile Main question Evidence to keep
Steady load Does the intended workflow remain usable under a typical busy-period mix? Stable admission, caller-side delay, correct writes, and available fallback.
Ramp Which dependency becomes constrained as arrivals or occupancy grow? The first point where queue age, rejected traffic, or timing begins to change.
Spike Can the route absorb a brief wave or turn it into an approved alternative? Time of the burst, admission disposition, what callers heard, and how the backlog cleared.
Stress What fails first within the approved test ceiling? Bottleneck evidence, stop condition, and the behavior of safety and business gates.
Soak Does sustained work reveal slow leaks or accumulating problems? Session cleanup, worker rotation, queue behavior, retries, and resource usage over time.
Recovery What happens after a bottleneck clears? Restored service, drained or resolved work, and reconciliation of uncertain writes.

Observe the first constrained dependency

A carrier can queue, decline, or accept calls even as the application has room. A media gateway may accept sessions but start dropping audio under pressure. A model provider can limit simultaneous sessions or requests. An application programming interface (API) can throttle lookups while workers and phone lines remain available. Test enough of the path to show which decision failed first.

Session Initiation Protocol (SIP) is one way to establish the call. For SIP load testing, capture signaling and media separately. An accepted invitation can be followed by an unusable or delayed audio stream. Compare setup messages, receiving media, and completed tasks. For call center load testing, add receiving queue disposition and the outcome staff saw. The IVR and conversational routing test guide explains what to verify at the destination. IETF RFC 3261

Report latency as callers experience it

Keep a timeline for caller turn end, agent playback start, tool attempts, queue entry, and terminal outcome. Compare the center and slower end of the latency distribution. The average can improve when the slowest attempts time out and drop out of a completed-call view. Report admitted, rejected, abandoned, and timed-out attempts alongside completed-call timing. Google’s Site Reliability Engineering guidance pairs latency and errors with traffic and saturation for diagnosis. Google SRE monitoring guidance

Distinguish the turn delay from call setup, queue wait, and a downstream write. A slow lookup can cause a long silent turn while the phone remains connected. An opening prompt can sound fast while abandoned calls accumulate elsewhere. Use the voice AI latency guide to define observable boundaries for caller-perceived delay.

Check how the system limits work

Backpressure gives upstream components a signal to slow, stop accepting, or defer work when a downstream dependency cannot keep up. For a live voice turn, a long queue can be worse than an early, approved fallback. Decide which work can wait, such as a post-call summary, and which work needs an immediate caller response. Avoid unbounded retries. They can keep a dependency busy after the initial traffic wave has passed. Google SRE overload guidance

Use an agreed recovery path. If a calendar times out after an appointment may already have been saved, the system can read the outcome using the original operation identifier. When the outcome remains uncertain, acknowledge a pending request and apply the business’s handoff rule. Verify recovery at the caller and backend boundaries. A clear queue does not resolve a duplicate appointment that was created during the incident.

Inspect the behavior of concurrent AI voice calls

In voice AI performance testing, a slow call can change the amount of work the system is holding open. Active streams, outstanding lookups, and waiting callers can each accumulate in a different place. The result should identify the first place that stopped keeping up, the fallback that callers heard, and the point at which routine operations recovered.

In voice AI stress testing, set the objective before adding pressure. For example, a route owner can look for safe admission control, while a workflow owner verifies that the system never confirms an unverified reservation. That distinction makes voice agent concurrency an operational finding the business can act on.

B2B Voice Load Testing Framework

Load testing stages and acceptance evidence
Stage What to do Practical acceptance evidence
Scope Agree on authorized endpoints, allowed traffic, relevant provider limits, workflow mix, and stop conditions. Owners can identify the exact route, versions, limits, and permitted responses.
Establish a reference Exercise each route and workflow at a known working profile first. A listener hears usable media, business actions are verified, and instrumentation captures each attempt.
Vary pressure Run one defined load profile at a time before combining conditions. The record identifies changes in arrivals, occupancy, dependency saturation, and caller outcomes.
Test a dependency Introduce a controlled slowdown or an approved failure at one sandbox boundary. The expected fallback runs and prohibited writes remain absent.
Observe recovery Remove the pressure, wait for the system to stabilize, and reconcile in-flight work. Queues return to the expected state and authoritative systems show the correct final records.
Report scope Record the actual route, test profile, configured version, observed limits, and visibility gaps. A reader can tell which scenario worked and what remains unknown.

Checklist before reporting a usable workload

  • Every offered attempt has a traceable admission or terminal disposition, or an explicitly recorded measurement gap.
  • No integrity gate failed, including any customer-defined rule against false confirmations or duplicate writes.
  • The delay and failure behavior stayed within the workflow’s agreed criteria.
  • A tested fallback operated when required and reached a verified destination or authorized state.
  • The system returned to the expected operating condition and the team reconciled uncertain actions. The guide to voice agent reliability describes fallback and recovery evidence.

A fictional example of a misleading load test

A scheduling team replays clean calls through an internal voice endpoint. The model responds promptly and the tool stub instantly returns availability. The team then exercises the authorized phone route with a sandbox calendar that sometimes takes longer. It sees more calls stay open, a queue grow, and an agent announce time slots before a lookup has completed.

The first run provided evidence about the selected voice and application path under its test conditions. The second run exposed a dependency the first bypassed. During the repeat test, check offered and admitted calls, the calendar’s status, actual playout, and whether the caller heard the approved pending message. After the traffic eases, confirm that temporary queues clear and the booking store contains only the intended records. A single load test is evidence for its recorded scope. Frequently asked questions

Is calls per second the same as simultaneous calls?

Calls per second describes the rate of attempts or starts at a specified boundary. Concurrency describes how many calls or sessions are active at the same time. Track both and document where the system counted them.

Can average concurrency tell me the peak?

Average concurrency describes occupancy across the selected window. Use event traces or adequately granular observations to identify the observed peak and capture brief bursts.

What should I test when the calendar or CRM slows down?

Check how the agent responds while the dependency is slow, whether queues or retries accumulate, and what happens after recovery. Verify the final record independently.

What makes a voice load test result reusable?

Record the route, workload, versions, dependencies, start and end definitions, acceptance criteria, and any missing evidence. Compare a later run under equivalent conditions before attributing a change.
